## What Rarámuri Digital is

**Rarámuri Digital** is a research infrastructure that publishes and transforms Rarámuri–Spanish lexicographic data into **traceable, versioned, citable, interoperable, and reproducible** research objects. The project explicitly separates documentary evidence, working representations, computational normalization, derived products, and linguistic validation.

It is not an orthographic standard or an authority for all Rarámuri varieties. Technical availability does not constitute linguistic or community validation. Forms and analyses must be interpreted according to their provenance and validation state.

## Kenneth Simon Hilton and the Samachique source

The principal historical lexicographic reference associated with the project is **Kenneth Simon Hilton**, also catalogued as Kenneth S. Hilton and K. Simón Hilton. His *Diccionario tarahumara de Samachique, Chihuahua, México* was issued in **1993** as a special corrected and updated edition by the Instituto Lingüístico de Verano. Its archival record describes **viii + 146 pages**, approximately **2,500 entries**, Rarámuri–Spanish and Spanish–Rarámuri sections, grammatical categories, multiple senses, derived forms, examples, and notes. The subject language is identified as **Central Tarahumara**, associated with Samachique.

The documentary history predates 1993. El Colegio de México preserves *Tarahumara y español* (1959), compiled by Kenneth Simon Hilton with **Ramón López B. and Emiliano Carrasco T.** as collaborators. In 2016, SIL Mexico archived Wes Shoemaker's *Diccionario tarahumara actualizado*, described as an update of VIMSA 101 (1993) with recent spelling changes and catalogued as a draft without peer review.

Rarámuri Digital **does not conflate this bibliographic genealogy with its production pipeline**. `SRC-01` identifies Hilton 1993 as a reference and collation source; `SRC-02` identifies the structured working PDF actually consumed by the extraction script. Until the edition identity of `SRC-02` is demonstrated documentary evidence, the dataset is not described as a direct transcription of Hilton 1993.

## Coverage

- 2,581 lexical entries with persistent `RD-######` identifiers.
- Lemma, source form, normalized form, and homonym number.
- Grammatical classification and category family.
- Translation, senses, examples, variants, and source comments.
- Source code, document, pages, and transcription status.
- 30 derived products: corpora, inventories, variants, indexes, thesaurus, ontology, and traceability.

## Reproducibility

Node.js 22.13 or later is required.

```bash
npm install
npm run data:exports
npm run data:quality
npm run validate
npm run dev
```

CI goes beyond application build checks: it tests deterministic derived layers, validates CLDF, validates TEI Lex-0 against the official schema, regenerates quality reports, runs deep corpus audits, and checks reproducible exports. See [`.github/workflows/validate.yml`](.github/workflows/validate.yml).

## 🧭 Linguistic rights and governance

Indigenous Peoples have the right to preserve, revitalize, use, develop, and transmit their languages to future generations. This right is recognized by [Article 13 of the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/606782?ln=en) and, in Mexico, by the [General Law on the Linguistic Rights of Indigenous Peoples](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LGDLPI.pdf).

This infrastructure is intended to support Rarámuri documentation, consultation, and teaching. It does not replace the linguistic, cultural, or political authority of communities and speakers. Data reuse must retain attribution and provenance, prevent appropriation and decontextualization, respect community decisions and restrictions, and support effective Rarámuri participation in corpus validation, correction, and governance.
